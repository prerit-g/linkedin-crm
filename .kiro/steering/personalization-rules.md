# Personalization Rules

## What Personalization Is Allowed

Personalization must be FACTUAL. Only use information that is explicitly present in the
profile data provided by the user.

### Allowed Sources

- First name (from profile)
- Current company (from profile)
- Current job title (from profile)
- Function derived from job title (using classification rules)
- Seniority derived from job title (using classification rules)
- Clearly stated professional specialization (from headline or about section)
- Clearly visible hiring context (e.g., profile says "Currently hiring junior roles")

### Function Priority for Personalization

Choose the most specific function the profile supports:

1. Accounts Payable → "Accounts Payable"
2. Accounts Receivable → "Accounts Receivable"
3. Procure-to-Pay → "P2P" or "Procure-to-Pay"
4. Order-to-Cash → "O2C" or "Order-to-Cash"
5. Record-to-Report → "R2R" or "Record-to-Report"
6. Accounting → "Accounting"
7. Finance Operations → "Finance Operations"
8. Finance → "finance" (generic, lowercase when used mid-sentence)
9. FinTech → "FinTech"

Examples:
- "Accounts Payable Associate" → use "Accounts Payable", not "finance"
- "P2P Operations Associate" → use "P2P"
- "Finance Operations Manager" → use "Finance Operations"
- "Finance Manager" (no specific sub-function) → use "finance"

## What Must NEVER Be Invented

Never fabricate or infer beyond what the profile states:

- Mutual connections
- Prior conversations or meetings
- Referrals from third parties
- Hiring status (unless profile explicitly states it)
- Open roles (unless profile explicitly states it)
- Team leadership responsibility (unless title/description explicitly states it)
- Skills not listed on the profile
- Experience not listed on the profile
- Company information not provided by the user
- Relationships (e.g., "I saw you connected with X")

## Prohibited Phrases

Do not use artificial compliments or exaggerated flattery:

PROHIBITED:
- "I was extremely impressed by your profile."
- "I've been following your amazing career."
- "Your incredible journey inspired me."
- "You are clearly a leader in your field."
- "I've admired your work for a long time."
- Any similar hollow flattery

These phrases sound AI-generated and undermine credibility.

## Prohibited AI-Sounding Language

Avoid generic, padded, or AI-sounding phrases:

PROHIBITED:
- "I hope this message finds you well."
- "I wanted to reach out to you today."
- "I am passionate about leveraging synergies."
- "As someone who is deeply committed to excellence..."
- "I believe we could create mutual value."
- Any similar corporate filler

## Tone Requirements

- Concise professional networking tone
- Direct and purposeful
- Respectful without being obsequious
- First-person active voice
- Natural English — read it as a human would write it

## Leadership Claim Rules

Do NOT use leadership language unless the title explicitly supports it.

SAFE:
- "AP Manager" → "I see you lead the Accounts Payable team"
- "Finance Operations Manager" → "I see you lead the Finance Operations team"

UNSAFE (do not use unless title supports it):
- "Finance Analyst" → do NOT say "I see you lead the finance team"
- "Senior Associate" → do NOT say "I see you manage the team"

If leadership is unclear: fall back to Category 1 phrasing or use Manual Review.

## Hiring Claim Rules

Do NOT assert that someone is hiring unless their profile explicitly states it.

SAFE phrasing (conditional):
- "...wanted to see if you are actively hiring or building a pipeline for junior roles."
- "If your team has any junior openings or an internal referral program..."

UNSAFE phrasing (do not use):
- "Since you are currently hiring for junior roles..."
- "I saw you posted a job for an AP Analyst..."

Unless the profile input explicitly contains hiring information, use the conditional phrasing
from the templates.
